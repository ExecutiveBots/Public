# 10 K

> Source: <https://github.com/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/10-K>

10K-SKILL

**Domain-Specialty SKILL (Canonical) — Form 10-K Annual Periodic Report Orchestration**

This is the **canonical specialty SKILL** governing Bill's orchestration of Form 10-K annual periodic-report filings. The 10-K is the bench's most comprehensive periodic deliverable: one filing per fiscal year on a 60 / 75 / 90-day window per filer status (LAF / AF / SRC-NAF), with PCAOB AS 2201 integrated audit (reasonable assurance), AS 3101 Critical Audit Matters in the auditor's report, §404(b) ICFR auditor attestation for LAF and AF (SRC and EGC exempt), Reg S-X full annual financial statements (three-year income; two-year balance sheet), the full Reg S-K Item 402 executive-compensation package (CD&A, Pay-Versus-Performance, CEO Pay Ratio, clawback recovery, option-timing), and the Part III incorporation-by-reference window from the definitive proxy filed within 120 days of fiscal year-end.

This file augments [Bill-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-SKILL) (general CEO orchestration) and operates within the broader cycle architecture specified in [Periodic-Report-Assembly-Playbook](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Periodic-Report-Assembly-Playbook) (which orchestrates both 10-K and 10-Q cycles). Where [Periodic-Report-Assembly-Playbook](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Periodic-Report-Assembly-Playbook) establishes the master T-90 through T+5 milestone architecture, this file specifies the **10-K-distinctive disciplines**: AS 2201 vs. AS 4105 distinction, §404(b) ICFR attestation, full-form Reg S-X presentation, Part III IBR mechanics, Item 402 cascade, and annual-cycle exception paths.

> ## ⚖ TAKE NOTICE
>
> >
> Form 10-K is signed by the Human CEO and Human CFO under the Rule 13a-14(a) / 15d-14(a) certifications, with corresponding SOX § 906 (18 U.S.C. § 1350) certifications carrying criminal penalties for knowing violation, **plus** a majority of the board of directors signs the registration statement / report itself under Form 10-K General Instruction D(2)(a) — the AI agents on the bench produce the assembly substrate, **not** the certifications. The 10-K is **audited**, not reviewed: PCAOB AS 2201 provides **reasonable assurance** (opinion form), and for LAF / AF filers the auditor also opines on ICFR effectiveness under § 404(b) — SRC and EGC are statutorily exempt from § 404(b) (15 U.S.C. § 7262(c); JOBS Act § 103). AS 3101 Critical Audit Matters appear in the audit report for non-EGC filers since FY ending June 30, 2019, and for all EGC filers since FY ending Dec. 15, 2020. The 10-K is the integration point for the cycle's *Caremark* / *Marchand* defense package — the annual oversight evidence base — under the *In re McDonald's*, 289 A.3d 343 (Del. Ch. 2023), officer-level extension. See [Take-Notice](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Take-Notice).

## §0 Honesty Above All

This SKILL operates beneath the [Bill-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-SKILL) § 0 Honesty Above All constraint. No 10-K-cycle output this skill produces — close-cycle reconciliation, MD&A draft, risk-factor full update, full financial-statement draft, ICFR management assessment, Item 402 compensation package, controls-and-procedures evaluation, or filing certification package — may include false statements, omit material facts whose omission would render disclosed facts misleading, or contribute to AI-washing posture per *In re Delphia (USA) Inc. & Global Predictions Inc.*, Admin. Proc. File No. 3-21894 (Mar. 18, 2024).

The 10-K is the **definitive** annual disclosure. It carries Securities Act § 11 strict-liability exposure when incorporated by reference into a Form S-3 registration statement (the dominant pattern for follow-on offerings); Exchange Act § 10(b) / Rule 10b-5 fraud exposure; SOX § 302 / § 906 certification exposure; and full-board signature exposure under Form 10-K General Instruction D(2)(a). The aggregated exposure surface exceeds the quarterly-cycle exposure of any single 10-Q. Bill's orchestration treats the 10-K as the consolidation point for the year's cycle-by-cycle work; it does not tolerate "we already disclosed it in a 10-Q" as a substitute for the annual restatement of facts and risks.

## §1 Scope

### 1.1 What This SKILL Covers

The orchestration of Form 10-K annual periodic-report filings. Specifically:

- T-90 cycle architecture with per-phase milestone management (§4);
- PCAOB AS 2201 integrated audit coordination (vs. AS 4105 interim review for 10-Q) (§9);
- AS 3101 Critical Audit Matters disclosure in audit report (§9);
- § 404(b) ICFR auditor attestation for LAF / AF filers; § 404(a) management assessment for all filers (§13);
- Reg S-X full annual financial-statement compliance (Articles 5 / 7 / 9 commercial / insurance / banking, or Article 8 SRC) with three-year income / two-year balance-sheet comparative discipline (§10);
- Item 7 MD&A annual construction under Reg S-K Item 303(a) and (b) full annual scope (§11);
- Item 1A full risk-factor disclosure under Reg S-K Item 105 (§12);
- Item 1C cybersecurity annual program disclosure under Reg S-K Item 106 (§12);
- Item 9A controls and procedures with § 404(a) management assessment and § 404(b) auditor attestation (LAF / AF only) (§13);
- SOX § 302 / § 906 annual certification package (§14);
- Reg S-K Item 402 executive-compensation cascade — CD&A, summary table, grants, options, vesting, post-employment, director compensation, CEO pay ratio (Item 402(u)), pay-versus-performance (Item 402(v)), clawback recovery (Item 402(w)), option-grant timing (Item 402(x)) (§15);
- Part III item structure with definitive-proxy incorporation-by-reference window (120 days from FYE) (§7);
- Item 9B Other Information and Item 9C HFCAA Foreign Jurisdictions (§16);
- Cycle exception paths: NT 10-K under Form 12b-25 (15-calendar-day cure); mid-cycle Item 4.02 cascade; Item 1.05 reframing into annual Item 1C; Part III late-filing (10-K/A) (§16);
- [Governance-Gate](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Governance-Gate) authorization routing (§17);
- Audit-trail capture per [Audit-Trail-Spec](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Audit-Trail-Spec) § 3.5 four-record sequence pattern adapted for annual EDGAR transmission (§18);
- Single-principal subscriber adaptations per [Single-Principal-Subscriber-Appendix](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Single-Principal-Subscriber-Appendix).

### 1.2 What This SKILL Does Not Cover

- **Form 10-Q quarterly reports.** [Bill-10Q-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-10Q-SKILL) orchestrates 10-Q cycles. The cycle-by-cycle 10-Q outputs feed the 10-K consolidation.
- **Form 8-K current reports.** [Bill-8K-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-8K-SKILL) orchestrates 8-K filings (including any filed during the 10-K cycle).
- **Definitive proxy statement (Schedule 14A / 14C).** Outside-counsel-led; coordinated with 10-K cycle. The proxy is the IBR vehicle for Part III; cycle interlock detailed in §7 but proxy drafting itself is outside this SKILL. *See* [proxy-statement-14A-14C-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/proxy-statement-14A-14C-SKILL).
- **The substantive determination of accounting treatment.** That belongs to Tom + auditor; this SKILL orchestrates the determination workflow, not the substantive determination.
- **Form S-1 / S-3 / S-8 registration statements.** Outside counsel-led; Tom + James + outside counsel cycle. The 10-K is the IBR vehicle for S-3 forward incorporation; cycle interlock noted but registration drafting outside scope.

## §3 Filer Categories, Deadlines, and Cycle Calendar

### 3.1 Filing deadlines

| Filer category                  | 10-K deadline      | Days after FYE |
|---------------------------------|--------------------|----------------|
| Large Accelerated Filer (LAF)   | 60 calendar days   | T+60           |
| Accelerated Filer (AF)          | 75 calendar days   | T+75           |
| Smaller Reporting Company (SRC) | 90 calendar days   | T+90           |
| Non-Accelerated Filer (NAF)     | 90 calendar days   | T+90           |
| Emerging Growth Company (EGC)   | Per other category | Per other      |

Unlike the 10-Q (where LAF and AF share 40 days), the 10-K **does** differentiate LAF (60) from AF (75). SRC and NAF share 90 days.

### 3.2 §404(b) ICFR auditor attestation eligibility

| Filer category | § 404(a) management assessment | § 404(b) auditor attestation                |
|----------------|--------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| LAF            | ✓ Required                     | ✓ Required                                  |
| AF             | ✓ Required                     | ✓ Required                                  |
| SRC            | ✓ Required                     | ✗ Exempt (§ 404(c))                         |
| NAF            | ✓ Required                     | ✗ Exempt (§ 404(c))                         |
| EGC            | ✓ Required                     | ✗ Exempt for first 5 years (JOBS Act § 103) |

SRC was added to the § 404(b) exemption by the SEC's 2020 amendments (SEC Rel. No. 34-88365 (Mar. 12, 2020)) — the carve-out covers SRC with revenue \< \$100M.

### 3.3 Sample fiscal-year cycle calendar (calendar-year LAF)

| Year    | FYE          | 10-K due    | NT 10-K latest | Cure window expires | Proxy due (Part III IBR) |
|---------|--------------|-------------|----------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| FY 2025 | Dec 31, 2025 | Mar 2, 2026 | Mar 3, 2026    | Mar 17, 2026        | Apr 30, 2026             |
| FY 2026 | Dec 31, 2026 | Mar 1, 2027 | Mar 2, 2027    | Mar 16, 2027        | Apr 30, 2027             |

NT 10-K: filed within **1 business day** after due date; **15-calendar-day** cure window. Within cure: timely for S-3 / Rule 144. Outside cure: late filing; S-3 General Instr. I.A.3 eligibility lost for 12 months. *See* §16.1 and [NT-10K-NT-10Q-late-filing-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/NT-10K-NT-10Q-late-filing-SKILL).

**Note:** 15-calendar-day NT 10-K cure vs. 5-calendar-day NT 10-Q cure. The difference reflects the integrated-audit complexity and is the principal reason mid-cycle slippage is more often manageable in the annual cycle than in the quarterly cycle.

### 3.4 Cycle anchor

T = filing-due-date. T-90 is the standard cycle-open anchor (per [Periodic-Report-Assembly-Playbook](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Periodic-Report-Assembly-Playbook)). For LAF (60-day filer), T-90 falls ~30 days before FYE; for AF (75-day filer) T-90 falls ~15 days before FYE; for SRC / NAF (90-day filer) T-90 = FYE. Pre-FYE cycle elapse provides PBC-list circulation, audit planning, going-concern preliminary review, and disclosure committee scoping. *See* §4.

### 4.2 AF variant (75-day deadline)

Same milestone sequence; shift all milestones 15 days later relative to T. Pre-FYE positioning reduced from 30 to 15 days; post-FYE cycle elapse extends from 60 to 75 days.

### 4.3 SRC/NAF variant (90-day deadline)

Same milestone sequence; shift all milestones 30 days later relative to T. Pre-FYE positioning at T-90 = FYE; full 90 days post-FYE for close and file. § 404(b) attestation milestones (T-18 integration with auditor) omitted; § 404(a) management assessment retained.

### 4.4 Cycle compression discipline

Slippage at any milestone cascades. The non-negotiable boundary: § 302 / § 906 signatures cannot be wet-signed under time pressure that compromises the certifying officer's actual review, and the auditor cannot issue AS 2201 / AS 3101 opinions before completing substantive fieldwork. Where T-3 HBR review reveals substantive issue, the cycle slips. The fallback is NT 10-K + 15-calendar-day cure (§16.1) — **not** rushed filing of unverified content. *In re McDonald's* and the *Caremark* line position rushed annual oversight as the breach the officer-level *Caremark* extension addresses.

### 4.5 Pre-FYE positioning (T-90 through T-60)

The 30 days before FYE (for LAF) are reserved for:

- PBC (prepared-by-client) workpaper readiness and audit-evidence accumulation;
- AS 2110 audit-risk-assessment finalization with auditor;
- Account-by-account reconciliation discipline reset;
- Known-issue identification (impairment indicators; covenant compliance; tax provisioning; ASC 842 lease; ASC 815 derivative; ASC 740-10 / FIN 48 UTP);
- Outside-specialist engagement (valuation, tax, internal-audit);
- Disclosure committee membership and meeting cadence confirmation;
- Going-concern preliminary review;
- Subsequent-events evaluation framework set for T-0 evaluation through filing date.

## §9 PCAOB AS 2201 Annual Audit and ICFR Attestation

### 9.1 AS 2201 vs. AS 4105 — the critical distinction

| Dimension              | AS 2201 (10-K integrated audit)                                 | AS 4105 (10-Q review)              |
|------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Type of assurance      | **Reasonable** (positive form)                                  | **Limited** (negative form)        |
| Auditor's report       | Opinion (typically unqualified) on FS and ICFR                  | Standard review report; no opinion |
| Procedures             | Substantive testing + ICFR walkthroughs and testing             | Analytical procedures, inquiries   |
| ICFR audit             | § 404(b) attestation for LAF / AF                               | None                               |
| Materiality            | Annual materiality                                              | Same standard applied to interim   |
| Critical Audit Matters | Required under AS 3101 (non-EGC since 2017; all EGC since 2020) | Not applicable                     |
| Independence           | Same regime                                                     | Same regime                        |
| Output                 | Audit report filed in 10-K; § 404(b) opinion (LAF/AF)           | Review report typically not filed  |

### 9.2 § 404(a) management assessment

All filers (except SRC NAF with revenue \< \$100M, who remain subject to § 404(a) but exempt from § 404(b)):

- Establish and maintain ICFR;
- Conduct annual evaluation of ICFR effectiveness;
- Disclose framework used (typically COSO 2013);
- Issue management's report on ICFR in Item 9A;
- Identify any material weakness and disclose;
- Identify any unremediated material weakness from prior year and discuss progress.

### 9.3 § 404(b) auditor attestation (LAF / AF only)

Auditor performs integrated audit:

- Audit of FS under AS 3101 → opinion on FS;
- Audit of ICFR under AS 2201 → opinion on ICFR effectiveness;
- Two opinions issued in same audit report; can have different conclusions (e.g., unqualified FS opinion + adverse ICFR opinion due to material weakness). Material weakness in ICFR results in:
- Adverse opinion on ICFR;
- Item 9A management report discloses material weakness;
- § 302 / § 906 certifications "based on knowledge" still given but with material-weakness disclosure;
- Remediation plan disclosed;
- D&O insurance notice (per [D-and-O-insurance-claims-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/D-and-O-insurance-claims-SKILL)).

### 9.4 AS 3101 Critical Audit Matters

For non-EGC filers since FY ending June 30, 2019; for all EGC filers since FY ending Dec. 15, 2020. CAMs are matters communicated or required to be communicated to the audit committee that:

- Relate to accounts or disclosures material to FS;
- Involve especially challenging, subjective, or complex auditor judgment. Each CAM identified in audit report with description of:
- Why the matter was a CAM;
- How the matter was addressed in the audit;
- Reference to relevant FS accounts or disclosures. Common CAM categories: revenue recognition (ASC 606 estimates); business combinations (ASC 805 valuation); goodwill impairment (ASC 350); income taxes (ASC 740); credit losses (ASC 326 / CECL); going concern.

### 9.5 AS 2201 audit timing

Audit fieldwork begins at T-40 (per §4.1) and runs through T-10 audit committee meeting. Pre-FYE planning at T-75 sets risk assessment; substantive procedures and ICFR testing run T-40 through T-15; engagement-quality review T-12 through T-5; opinion issuance T-3 through T-2 (concurrent with HBR review).

### 9.6 Auditor disagreement during AS 2201

If auditor concludes material modification needed and management disagrees, escalation path:

1.  AS 1301 communication to audit committee;
2.  Audit committee resolution;
3.  If unresolved, auditor cannot issue unqualified opinion;
4.  Practical resolution: management modification; or NT 10-K + continued investigation; or Item 4.02 evaluation (if prior-period FS implicated); or qualified / adverse opinion (rare). Cycle architecture position: audit-committee meeting at T-10 is the resolution forum. Late-cycle disagreement is the principal driver of NT 10-K filings.

### 11.3 The "silence-is-affirmation" trap (annual)

Same principle as 10-Q (per [Bill-10Q-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-10Q-SKILL) §10.3) intensified for annual MD&A. Every known trend disclosed in the prior 10-K MD&A must be either:

- Affirmed as continuing without material change; or
- Updated to reflect actual change. Silence on a trend that has materially changed = §10(b) exposure under *Stratte-McClure v. Morgan Stanley*, 776 F.3d 94 (2d Cir. 2015). The annual MD&A drafting protocol: each prior 10-K known trend reviewed; status documented; non-updates flagged as conscious affirmation, not oversight.

*Macquarie Infrastructure Corp. v. Moab Partners*, 601 U.S. 257 (2024) limits Rule 10b-5(b) pure-omission liability — the omission must render an affirmative statement misleading. But Item 303 itself creates an affirmative disclosure duty; failure to disclose material known trends is a 303 violation, with §10(b) exposure flowing through *Stratte-McClure*.

### 11.4 Critical accounting estimates discipline

Item 303(b)(3) requires:

- Identification of estimates that require subjective and complex judgments;
- Why the estimate is subject to uncertainty;
- How management developed the estimate;
- Sensitivity to changes in assumptions (quantitative where reasonably available). Not boilerplate. Typical critical estimates for micro-cap subscribers: revenue (ASC 606 stand-alone selling price; variable consideration); credit losses (ASC 326 CECL); goodwill / long-lived asset impairment indicators; deferred-tax-asset valuation allowance; stock-based compensation valuation (ASC 718 — Black-Scholes / Monte Carlo); derivative liability fair value (ASC 815-15 / 815-40); revenue contingencies; ASC 740-10 / FIN 48 uncertain tax positions.

### 11.5 Going-concern MD&A integration

Where ASC 205-40 evaluation identifies Rung 2 or higher:

- Detailed MD&A description of conditions raising substantial doubt;
- Management plans to mitigate (financing pipeline; cost reduction; asset monetization);
- Probability assessment of plans being effectively implemented (Rung 2) or not effectively mitigating doubt (Rung 3);
- Cross-reference to footnote disclosure;
- Risk-factor update in Item 1A. Per [going-concern-ASU-2014-15-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/going-concern-ASU-2014-15-SKILL) §§5–6.

- MD&A discussion of financial impact, ongoing remediation costs, insurance recovery;

## §12 Item 1A Risk Factors and Item 1C Cybersecurity Program

### 12.1 Item 1A — Reg S-K Item 105

Full annual disclosure of material factors that make an investment in the issuer's securities speculative or risky. Standards post-SEC Rel. No. 33-10825 (Mar. 2020):

- Material risks specific to the issuer, not generic boilerplate;
- Organized under appropriate sub-headings;
- Summary risk-factor section (Item 105(b)) required if total Item 1A exceeds **15 pages**;
- Each risk factor describes the risk in plain English; consequences if it materializes. Generic boilerplate (e.g., "our business is subject to economic conditions") not sufficient. Specificity is the test — *In re Marvell Tech.* securities-class-action settlements illustrate the failure mode.

### 12.2 Annual update discipline

Each prior 10-K risk factor reviewed:

- Carry forward unchanged where still material;
- Update to reflect changes in scope, probability, or consequence;
- Remove where no longer material (rare); document removal reasoning in working file;
- Add new risk factors for matters that emerged during FY (new litigation, regulatory developments, cybersecurity incidents, restatements, going-concern, business model changes).

### 12.3 Typical micro-cap risk-factor inventory

- Going-concern substantial doubt;
- Need for additional financing; dilution;
- Limited operating history;
- Customer concentration;
- Reliance on key personnel;
- Smaller-reporting-company exemptions (§ 404(b), reduced executive compensation disclosure);
- Penny-stock-rule application (Rule 3a51-1) and broker-dealer suitability;
- Reverse-merger history (where applicable);
- Restatement and material-weakness disclosure (if applicable);
- Toxic financing terms (death-spiral convertibles);
- Cybersecurity (where Item 1C / Item 1.05 has been triggered);
- Litigation exposure (specific matters);
- Regulatory exposure (specific regulators);
- §16 insider sales / dilution risk;
- HFCAA exposure (where applicable).

### 12.4 Cybersecurity in Item 1A

Generic "we face cybersecurity risk" insufficient. Specific risk factors include:

- Reliance on specific systems / vendors and consequences of compromise;
- Prior incidents experienced and ongoing remediation;
- Regulatory exposure (state AG; HIPAA; GLBA; FTC Section 5);
- Customer / contractual obligations potentially breached.

### 12.5 Item 1C — Cybersecurity Annual Program (Reg S-K Item 106)

Three required disclosures per SEC Rel. No. 33-11216 (July 2023):

**(b)(1) Risk management and strategy:**

- Description of processes to assess, identify, and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats;
- Whether and how integrated into overall ERM;
- Whether and how third-party providers / consultants used;
- Whether processes designed to address risks from third-party service providers. **(b)(2) Effects:**
- Any cybersecurity incidents or risks that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition;
- Cross-reference to Item 1.05 8-K filed during FY for any material incident. **(c)(1) Governance:**
- Board oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats;
- Specific board committee with cybersecurity oversight (if any) and its composition;
- Processes for board / committee to be informed of incidents;
- Management's role and expertise in assessing and managing material cybersecurity risks. Per [cybersecurity-incident-1.05-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/cybersecurity-incident-1.05-SKILL) §11.

The annual program disclosure is Robert's principal annual disclosure deliverable; Robert maintains the FY incident log throughout the year and presents inventory at T-28.

## §13 Item 9A — Controls and Procedures

### 13.1 § 404(a) management assessment

Required for all filers (no SRC / EGC exemption). Components:

- Statement of management's responsibility for establishing and maintaining adequate ICFR;
- Statement identifying the framework used (typically COSO 2013);
- Management's assessment of ICFR effectiveness as of FYE;
- Statement that auditor has issued an attestation report (for LAF / AF) **or** a statement that the report is not required (for SRC / NAF / EGC under § 404(c)).

### 13.2 Management's assessment process

- Identify significant accounts, disclosures, and assertions;
- Identify relevant entity-level controls (control environment; risk assessment; information & communication; monitoring per COSO);
- Identify transaction-level controls (process-level controls; ITGCs);
- Evaluate design and operating effectiveness;
- Aggregate deficiencies; classify as deficiency / significant deficiency / material weakness;
- Conclude on ICFR effectiveness.

### 13.3 § 404(b) auditor attestation (LAF / AF)

Auditor performs separate procedures under AS 2201:

- Top-down risk-based approach;
- Identification and testing of entity-level controls;
- Walkthroughs of key transaction classes;
- Tests of design effectiveness;
- Tests of operating effectiveness;
- Evaluation of identified deficiencies;
- Issuance of opinion on ICFR effectiveness. Adverse opinion on ICFR if material weakness exists at FYE — irrespective of remediation efforts in progress.

### 13.4 DC&P annual conclusion

Reg S-K Item 308(b) annual evaluation:

- CEO and CFO conclude on DC&P effectiveness as of FYE;
- Conclusion stated in Item 9A;
- §302 certification (Exhibit 31) mirrors Item 9A DC&P conclusion;
- Disclosure of any material change in ICFR during the fourth fiscal quarter (Q4-specific) plus changes for the FY overall (per most filer practice).

### 13.5 Material weakness disclosure

If material weakness exists:

- Item 9A discloses nature of weakness;
- Disclosure of remediation plan and status;
- §302 certification "based on knowledge" continues but with caveat noting material weakness;
- §906 certification unaffected by material weakness (§906 is fairly-presents criterion, not effectiveness);
- Item 1A risk-factor update;
- Audit committee oversight documentation;
- D&O insurance notice (per [D-and-O-insurance-claims-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/D-and-O-insurance-claims-SKILL));
- Often triggers securities-class-action exposure under §10(b);
- Often triggers SEC inquiry.

### 13.6 Item 4.02 cascade impact on Item 9A

Where Item 4.02 has been filed during FY (or in connection with the 10-K cycle restatement):

- Item 9A ICFR conclusion typically transitions to "not effective due to material weakness in \[area\]";
- Material weakness specifically identified;
- §404(b) attestation (LAF / AF) likely adverse;
- Restatement disclosure cross-referenced. Per [restatement-and-item-4.02-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/restatement-and-item-4.02-SKILL) §§5–9; [internal-controls-SOX-404-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/internal-controls-SOX-404-SKILL) §§9–11.

## §14 SOX § 302 / § 906 Annual Certification Package

### 14.1 § 302 certification (Exhibits 31.1 and 31.2)

Same five-prong content as 10-Q (per [Bill-10Q-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-10Q-SKILL) §12.1) applied to the annual report:

1.  Reviewed the report;
2.  Based on knowledge, no untrue statement / no misleading omission;
3.  FS and other financial information fairly present;
4.  Responsible for DC&P and ICFR; designed and evaluated effectiveness; disclosed conclusions; disclosed changes in ICFR during last fiscal quarter (and FY overall);
5.  Disclosed to auditor and audit committee: significant deficiencies and material weaknesses; any fraud involving management or other employees with significant ICFR role.

### 14.2 § 906 certification (Exhibits 32.1 and 32.2)

18 U.S.C. § 1350: criminal certification. Standard text adapted for annual report. Knowing false certification: \$1M fine and 10-year imprisonment; willful: \$5M, 20 years.

### 14.3 Annual certification weight

The 10-K § 302 / § 906 certifications carry distinct weight because:

- They are made after the AS 2201 audit (vs. AS 4105 review for 10-Q) — auditor reasonable-assurance opinion provides foundation;
- They cover the full FY (vs. one quarter);
- They incorporate the § 404(a) management assessment of ICFR (and § 404(b) attestation for LAF / AF);
- The 10-K is the IBR vehicle for any subsequent S-3 / S-8 registration → § 11 Securities Act liability flows through;
- Annual certification is the principal evidence base for *In re McDonald's* officer-level *Caremark* duties.

### 14.4 Sub-certification chain (annual)

Patricia administers an expanded sub-certification cycle for the 10-K:

- T-12: Sub-cert request distributed to process owners; expanded scope vs. 10-Q includes business-unit certifications, IT GC certifications, legal-counsel certifications;
- T-7: Sub-certs returned;
- T-5: Aggregation review with Tom and James; deficiency analysis;
- T-3: Final sub-cert package supports CEO/CFO §302 certification. The annual sub-cert package is the most comprehensive of the year; serves as the foundation for the annual *Caremark*-defense package per [Bill-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-SKILL) § 5.4.

### 14.5 Form 10-K signature page (Gen Instr D(2)(a))

Beyond §302/§906 certifications, the Form 10-K itself is signed by:

- The issuer (one principal officer signing on behalf of issuer);
- Principal executive officer (PEO);
- Principal financial officer (PFO);
- Principal accounting officer (PAO) / controller;
- **Majority of the board of directors**. Each director signs individually (or under power of attorney filed as Exhibit 24). Single-principal subscriber adaptations per [Single-Principal-Subscriber-Appendix](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Single-Principal-Subscriber-Appendix).

The board-signature requirement exposes each signing director to § 11 strict-liability when the 10-K is incorporated by reference into a Form S-3 — this is the most direct director liability surface in periodic reporting.

### 15.2 CD&A (Item 402(b)) — non-SRC

- Objectives of compensation program;
- What the program is designed to reward;
- Each element of compensation;
- Why each element is paid;
- How each element fits into overall objectives. Plain English. Forward-looking statements about future compensation usually omitted (PSLRA safe harbor uncertain).

### 15.3 Summary Compensation Table (Item 402(c))

| Column                      | Content                                                                  |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Name and Principal Position | Each NEO                                                                 |

### 15.4 Pay Versus Performance (Item 402(v))

Effective for proxies filed for FYs ending after Dec. 16, 2022. Five-year tabular disclosure (SRC: three-year):

| Year | Total Compensation per Summary Table | "Compensation Actually Paid" | Total Shareholder Return (TSR) | Peer-Group TSR | Net Income | Company-Selected Measure |
|------|--------------------------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|----------------|------------|--------------------------|

"Compensation Actually Paid" — recomputation under Item 402(v) adjusting stock and option awards from grant-date fair value to mark-to-market valuation changes; includes pension service cost; excludes change in pension value not service-related.

Required narrative discussion of relationship between executive compensation actually paid and TSR / peer-group TSR / net income / Company-Selected Measure (CSM). CSM is a financial performance measure the issuer believes most strongly links exec comp to financial performance.

### 15.5 CEO Pay Ratio (Item 402(u)) — non-SRC

Disclosure of:

- Annual total compensation of CEO;
- Median annual total compensation of all employees other than CEO;
- Ratio of the two. Methodology for identifying median employee; permitted statistical sampling. Disclosure of methodology and any exclusions (foreign workforce excluded under conditions). Effective FY 2017 and forward.

### 15.6 Clawback Recovery Disclosure (Item 402(w))

Triggered when issuer has been required to prepare an accounting restatement and the issuer's clawback policy under Rule 10D-1 has been applied. Disclosure:

- Date restatement was required;
- Aggregate dollar amount of erroneously awarded compensation attributable to the restatement;
- Aggregate amount of erroneously awarded compensation that remains outstanding (not yet recovered);
- Forfeiture decisions by executive officers;
- Amounts recovered. Tabular format encouraged. Per [restatement-and-item-4.02-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/restatement-and-item-4.02-SKILL) §10; [item-402-executive-compensation-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/item-402-executive-compensation-SKILL) §§7–10.

### 15.7 Option-Grant Timing (Item 402(x))

Required for FYs ending after April 1, 2023. Disclosure:

- (a)(1) Policies and practices regarding timing of awards of options, SARs, similar options and stock awards in relation to disclosure of MNPI;
- (a)(2) Whether (and how) board / compensation committee took into account MNPI when determining timing and terms of awards;
- (a)(3) Whether (and how) issuer timed disclosure of MNPI for the purpose of affecting executive comp value. Tabular disclosure of awards made within four business days before or one business day after MNPI disclosure, including grant-date fair value vs. closing market price on grant date and 14th business day after MNPI disclosure.

Companion to Item 408(b) insider-trading policy disclosure.

### 15.8 Cycle integration

Item 402 work is the most time-intensive Part III element. Patricia + Tom + compensation committee coordinate from T-22 (cycle commencement of comp work) through T-7 (final approval). Pay-versus-Performance computation requires recomputation of "compensation actually paid" each year — substantial accounting workpaper. Where Part III is being incorporated by reference from proxy, the work is duplicative with proxy preparation; cycle-architecture coordination avoids duplication.

## §16 Cycle Exception Paths

### 16.1 NT 10-K under Rule 12b-25

If the 10-K cannot be filed by the due date:

| Step | Detail                                                                                                |
|------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1    | Risk identified at T-5 or earlier; "unable without unreasonable effort or expense" standard evaluated |
| 2    | Form 12b-25 (NT 10-K) prepared with Part III substantive explanation                                  |
| 3    | NT filed within **1 business day** after original due date                                            |
| 4    | Cure window: **15 calendar days** from original due date                                              |
| 5    | Within cure: timely for S-3 General Instr. I.A.3 and Rule 144(c)(1)                                   |
| 6    | Outside cure: S-3 eligibility lost for 12 months; Rule 144 affiliate sales restricted                 |

NT 10-K Part III explanation must be substantive — generic "administrative delays" draws negative inference. Acceptable bases:

- Auditor unable to complete AS 2201 / § 404(b) attestation;
- Material accounting matter under evaluation;
- Restatement work in progress (cross-reference Item 4.02 8-K if filed);
- Subsidiary or equity-method investee FS unavailable. 15-calendar-day cure (vs. 5-day for NT 10-Q) reflects integrated-audit complexity. NT 10-K is more common than NT 10-Q. Per [NT-10K-NT-10Q-late-filing-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/NT-10K-NT-10Q-late-filing-SKILL) §3.

### 16.2 Item 4.02 cascade through 10-K cycle

If a non-reliance determination occurs during the 10-K cycle:

1.  Form 8-K Item 4.02(a) (or 4.02(b)) filed within 4 BD per [Bill-8K-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-8K-SKILL) §3.4;
2.  10-K deadline likely deferred via NT 10-K (15-day cure window often sufficient if matter resolvable within cure; longer matters → outside-cure filing);
3.  Concurrent restatement work: 10-K reflects restated prior-year FS (if comparative periods implicated); previously-filed quarterly 10-Q/A's may also be required;
4.  Item 9A ICFR conclusion typically transitions to "not effective due to material weakness in \[area\]";
5.  § 404(b) auditor attestation (LAF / AF) → adverse opinion;
6.  § 302 / § 906 certifications re-executed in restated 10-K reflecting material-weakness disclosure;
7.  Item 1A risk-factor update reflecting restatement, material weakness, clawback exposure, securities-class-action exposure;
8.  SOX § 304 and Rule 10D-1 clawback analysis launched;
9.  D&O insurance notice (per [D-and-O-insurance-claims-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/D-and-O-insurance-claims-SKILL) §5.1);
10. Item 402(w) clawback recovery disclosure in next proxy / 10-K (Item 402(w) is annual, not periodic);
11. Item 601(b)(97) Exhibit 97 clawback policy filed (already required since FY ending after Dec. 1, 2023 per Rule 10D-1 implementation).

### 16.3 Cybersecurity incident in FY (Item 1.05 → Item 1C integration)

Material cybersecurity incident during FY:

1.  Mid-cycle Item 1.05 8-K filed per [Bill-8K-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-8K-SKILL) §3.1 and [cybersecurity-incident-1.05-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/cybersecurity-incident-1.05-SKILL) §3;
2.  10-K integration:
    - Item 1C annual program disclosure references the incident and ongoing remediation;
    - Item 1A risk-factor update reflecting cyber exposure and remediation status;
    - Item 7 MD&A discussion of financial impact, ongoing remediation costs, insurance recovery;
    - Item 9A controls evaluation — whether incident reflects DC&P or ICFR deficiency;
    - ASC 450 loss-contingency footnote disclosure;
    - Going-concern reassessment if material to liquidity.

### 16.4 Auditor change during FY (Item 4.01 → Item 9 + Item 14)

If auditor changed during FY:

1.  Form 8-K Item 4.01 filed at time of change per [Bill-8K-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-8K-SKILL) §3.4;
2.  Exhibit 16 letter from former auditor filed within 10 BD of 8-K;
3.  New auditor conducts AS 2201 integrated audit; may require re-performance of prior procedures (additional fieldwork time);
4.  Item 9 of 10-K cross-references the 4.01 8-K;
5.  Item 14 principal accountant fees reflects fees for new auditor;
6.  10-K deadline at risk; NT 10-K likely; outside-cure filing possible.

### 16.5 Part III late-filing (proxy not filed within 120 days)

If definitive proxy is not filed within 120 days of FYE:

1.  10-K's Part III items cannot continue indefinite IBR;
2.  10-K/A amendment filed within 120 days containing full Part III disclosures;
3.  Definitive proxy when filed contains the same Part III content with disclaimer of reference to 10-K/A;
4.  S-3 incorporation-by-reference of 10-K/A picks up Part III content. Common micro-cap pattern: SRC / NAF subscribers with 90-day 10-K deadline have only 30 days for proxy preparation under the 120-day IBR window. Many elect to include Part III in original 10-K, avoiding IBR entirely; this adds approximately 30-40 pages to the 10-K.

### 16.6 Charter or bylaw amendment during FY (Item 5.03 / Item 9B)

Mid-FY charter / bylaw amendment (e.g., reverse stock split, authorized share increase, charter amendments for going-private transaction):

1.  Form 8-K Item 5.03 filed within 4 BD of effectiveness per [Bill-8K-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-8K-SKILL) §3.5;
2.  10-K integration:
    - EPS retroactive restatement for all periods presented (ASC 260);
    - Item 8 FS reflect post-amendment cap structure;
    - Item 1 business description updated;
    - Exhibit 3 (charter / bylaws) updated with amended versions;
    - FINRA Rule 6490 coordination per [corporate-actions-FINRA-6490-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/corporate-actions-FINRA-6490-SKILL);
    - Transfer-agent (Edith) execution.

*Form 10-K is the bench's most comprehensive periodic deliverable: one filing per fiscal year on a 60 / 75 / 90-day window per filer status (LAF / AF / SRC-NAF). Unlike the 10-Q, the 10-K is audited under PCAOB AS 2201 (reasonable assurance, opinion form), with AS 3101 Critical Audit Matters in the auditor's report; LAF and AF receive an integrated audit including § 404(b) ICFR attestation, while SRC, NAF, and EGC are statutorily exempt from § 404(b). Reg S-X full annual financial statements (three-year income / two-year balance sheet / three-year cash flow) replace the Article 10 condensed presentation of the 10-Q. The full Reg S-K Item 402 cascade — CD&A, Summary Compensation Table, Pay Versus Performance (Item 402(v)), CEO Pay Ratio (Item 402(u); SRC and EGC exempt), Clawback Recovery (Item 402(w)), Option-Grant Timing (Item 402(x)) — is the executive-compensation annual deliverable. Part III items can be incorporated by reference from the definitive proxy if filed within 120 days of FYE; otherwise included in the 10-K or filed in a 10-K/A. The Form 10-K is signed by issuer, CEO, CFO, CAO, and a majority of the board under General Instruction D(2)(a) — exposing each signing director to § 11 strict-liability when the 10-K is incorporated by reference into a Form S-3. The 10-K is the consolidation point for the year's quarterly cycle work and the principal evidence base for the *In re McDonald's* officer-level *Caremark* defense package. Bill orchestrates; specialists execute their own scope per the §§5–8 per-Item routing matrix; the §4 T-90 cycle architecture enforces milestone-by-milestone progress with audit-fieldwork interlock; the four-record EDGAR submission sequence per §18 makes filing execution tamper-evident; the Governance Gate authorization per §17 is the human-fiduciary-adoption checkpoint, with the board signature page as the apex of the human-fiduciary-adoption surface. The architecture is the same as [Bill-10Q-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-10Q-SKILL), [Bill-8K-SKILL](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Bill-8K-SKILL), and [Periodic-Report-Assembly-Playbook](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Periodic-Report-Assembly-Playbook) — orchestration without substantive override — applied to annual periodic disclosure at the highest exposure surface in the periodic-reporting regime.*
