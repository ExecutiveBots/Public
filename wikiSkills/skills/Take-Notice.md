# Take Notice

**The Bench-Wide AI-System Disclosure**

This is the **canonical, load-bearing disclosure** for Executive.Bots. It declares the operating model to investors, counterparties, regulators, and stakeholders. It is reproduced verbatim — *not paraphrased* — in every SOUL and SKILL file on the bench, and forms the basis for every subscriber's Reg S-K Item 101 (description of business) and Item 1A (risk factors) disclosure.

This file is the single source of truth. Modifications propagate to every file that includes it by reference. Modification requires **BR (Board-Required) authorization via [Governance-Gate](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Governance-Gate) category \#37**.

## §2 Why This Disclosure Matters

Three categorical reasons make Take Notice operationally non-negotiable:

### 2.1 Caremark Information-Systems Prong

*Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019), and *In re Caremark*, 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996), require board-level monitoring of mission-critical compliance. For an issuer whose executive functions are partially executed by autonomous AI agents, the *existence and visibility* of that operational model is itself a Caremark predicate. A subscriber whose disclosure is silent or misleading on the use of AI agents has, by that silence, created a categorical Caremark vulnerability. Take Notice is the first operational defense element. See [Defense-Posture](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Defense-Posture) § Caremark / *Marchand*.

### 2.2 AI-Washing Enforcement

*In re Delphia (USA) Inc. & Global Predictions Inc.*, Admin. Proc. File No. 3-21894 (March 18, 2024), established categorical liability for misrepresentation of AI capabilities to investors — combined \$400,000 civil penalties. Take Notice's conservative self-description ("not natural persons; no fiduciary capacity; no professional licensure") is the operational defense against AI-washing exposure: subscribers who reproduce Take Notice verbatim cannot be alleged to have *understated* the AI element of their operations.

### 2.3 Reg FD and Rule 10b-5 Anti-Fraud Floor

Regulation FD (17 C.F.R. §§ 243.100–103) and Rule 10b-5 (17 C.F.R. § 240.10b-5) establish the disclosure floor: every material fact about the issuer's operations must be disclosed accurately. For a subscriber issuer, the use of autonomous AI agents in executive functions is material per se — it implicates operational risk, technological risk, regulatory risk, and governance risk. Take Notice is the canonical statement that makes the disclosure complete. *In re DraftKings Inc.*, Exchange Act Rel. No. 101198 (Sept. 26, 2024), demonstrates that Reg FD enforcement is strict-liability — there is no good-faith carve-out for selective or omitted disclosure.

## §3 Subscriber-Specific Variants for Reg S-K Disclosure

Take Notice is the canonical text. For each subscriber issuer, James drafts a subscriber-specific variant that adapts the canonical text into Reg S-K Item 101 and Item 1A language during onboarding (per [Subscriber-Onboarding-Playbook](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Subscriber-Onboarding-Playbook) § 1.2) and outside securities counsel reviews. The variants vary only in the issuer-specific framing (company name, listing venue, fiscal year-end, NAICS); the substantive disclosure content is the verbatim block in § 1.

### 3.1 Reg S-K Item 101 (Description of Business) Template

> *\[Subscriber issuer name\]* operates its executive functions through a deployment of the Executive.Bots V5 platform — a system of eight named, role-differentiated artificial-intelligence agents (Bill, Chief Executive Officer; Tom, Chief Financial Officer; James, General Counsel; Patricia, Chief Compliance Officer; Lisa, Chief Operating Officer; Mary, Chief Marketing Officer / Investor Relations; Robert, Chief Technology Officer; Edith, Transfer Agency Liaison). The agents do not hold fiduciary capacity in *\[subscriber issuer name\]*. Every material corporate action — including every periodic and current SEC filing, every public communication, every insider-trading plan administered under Rule 10b5-1, every transmission to *\[subscriber's transfer agent\]* of record, and every action that could obligate *\[subscriber issuer name\]* — is queued for and authorized by the Human Board of Record of *\[subscriber issuer name\]* through a procedural surface (the "Governance Gate") that captures the Board's authorization and propagates it through a hash-chained, seven-year WORM-archived audit trail. Authority remains with *\[subscriber issuer name\]*'s Human Board of Record.

### 3.2 Reg S-K Item 1A (Risk Factors) Template

> **Operational risk: dependence on the Executive.Bots platform.** *\[Subscriber issuer name\]*'s executive functions are partially executed by an autonomous artificial-intelligence agent system. While the system's actions are gated through Human Board of Record authorization, *\[subscriber issuer name\]* is dependent on the platform's continuous availability, integrity of the audit trail, and timely human review of staged actions. Platform unavailability, audit-trail integrity failures, or human-review delays could materially affect *\[subscriber issuer name\]*'s ability to make timely SEC filings, respond to regulatory inquiries, or manage corporate actions, any of which could subject *\[subscriber issuer name\]* to enforcement action, civil liability, or reputational harm.
>
> **Technological risk: errors in autonomous AI systems.** AI agent outputs may contain errors not detected before Human Board authorization. While the platform's substantive-review architecture (per-agent SOUL and SKILL specifications, multi-agent reconciliation, audit-trail capture) is designed to make errors detectable and correctable, errors may nevertheless reach material disclosures, financial statements, or compliance determinations. Such errors could result in restatement under ASC 250, regulatory enforcement, or shareholder litigation.
>
> **Regulatory risk: evolving AI-governance posture.** SEC, FINRA, and state regulator guidance on AI use in public-company executive functions is developing. Future guidance, enforcement, or rulemaking could require modifications to *\[subscriber issuer name\]*'s use of the Executive.Bots platform, potentially imposing transition costs, operational disruption, or restrictions on continued use of the platform.
>
> **Governance risk: Caremark / Marchand monitoring obligations.** *\[Subscriber issuer name\]*'s Human Board of Record retains all fiduciary capacity, including the duty to monitor mission-critical compliance under *Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019), and *In re Caremark Int'l Inc. Derivative Litig.*, 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996). The board-level monitoring system that the Executive.Bots platform's audit trail and Governance Gate provide is designed to support that obligation, but a finding by a court that the monitoring system is inadequate could expose Board members to derivative liability.

### 3.3 Form 8-K Item 5.05 Implications

Where a subscriber adopts or amends its Code of Ethics (per Reg S-K Item 406) and the amendment relates to the use of the Executive.Bots platform in policy administration, Form 8-K Item 5.05 disclosure within 4 business days is required. James drafts the Item 5.05 language, outside counsel reviews, and Gate authorization (BR per [Governance-Gate](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Governance-Gate) category \#21 / \#22) precedes filing.

### 3.4 Schedule 14A Item 7 Implications

Where a subscriber's Audit Committee (where exists per Rule 10A-3) reviews the platform's compliance posture or where a subscriber-additive ethics tradition is approved per [Ethics-Foundations](/ExecutiveBots/Public/wiki/Ethics-Foundations) § 11.5, Schedule 14A proxy-statement disclosure may be required for the next annual meeting.
